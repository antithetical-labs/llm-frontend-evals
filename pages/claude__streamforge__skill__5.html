How a stream works

01

Attach the CLI

streamforge attach hooks your assistant and your editor. Prompts, responses, and diffs go out as structured events, not as a screen recording.

02

Build in the open

Viewers read the prompt before the model answers, then watch the patch land. Secrets, env files, and paths you exclude never leave your machine.

03

Anyone forks

Each event is a commit. A viewer can branch from the exact moment the approach split and push their version back into the stream's chat.

What you get

Prompt trail

Every prompt is searchable

Grep the full history of a stream for the phrasing that actually worked. Prompts are indexed next to the diff they produced and the tests that followed.

Fork points

Branch from any second

Scrub the timeline, find the commit before the refactor went sideways, and fork there. You get the repo, the conversation context, and the failing test.

Replay

Sessions replay at your pace

Missed the stream? Replay reconstructs it from events, so you can step commit by commit instead of scrubbing a video looking for the moment it clicked.

Open beta

Start broadcasting this week

Handles are first come, first served. Beta accounts stream unlimited hours, keep private repos private, and can turn any session into a public fork tree later.
