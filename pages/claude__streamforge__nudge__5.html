Three moving parts. That's the whole product.

Install the CLI, it mirrors your editor buffer, your assistant transcript and your git history into one synchronized timeline. Viewers scrub it like a video, because it is one.

01

Broadcast the loop, not the screen

forge live hooks your assistant and your repo. Prompts, responses, tool calls and diffs stream as structured events — searchable, copyable, not a blurry 1080p capture.

→
02

Viewers read along

The timeline shows what was asked, what came back, and what actually got committed. Rewind to any turn. Diff the model's suggestion against what the human kept.

→
03

Fork at any second

Hit F and you get a repo pinned to that commit, plus the full prompt history as a machine-readable log. Take the branch somewhere else and stream your version.

02 —

Built for people who ship on Tuesdays.

Not a course platform. Not a webinar. A place to watch competent people flail, recover, and commit — at the speed it actually happens.

⌥

Prompt archaeology

Every stream leaves a transcript indexed by intent, model, file and outcome. Search "prompt that fixed an N+1 in Prisma" and get the exact turn, plus the diff that followed.

2.4M turns indexed · filter by model, language, or "human rejected it"
⑃

Forkable timeline

A fork isn't a zip file. You get the git history, the dependency lockfile, the container spec and the conversation state, so your assistant picks up exactly where theirs left off.

one keystroke → repo + prompt log + devcontainer, cold start in ~9s
◎

Secrets stay home

A local redaction pass strips env vars, tokens, customer strings and anything matching your .forgeignore before a single byte leaves the machine. Panic key kills the feed in 200ms.

client-side filtering · 15-second broadcast delay you control
